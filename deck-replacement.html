Know When to Replace

Signs Your Deck Needs Full Replacement

We get called to look at a lot of decks that homeowners assume just need some boards swapped out. In our experience with Licking County homes, the bigger issue is usually below the surface — in the ledger connection, the posts, or the rim joists. Once the framing goes, you're spending repair dollars on a structure that's borrowed time.

"When customers ask us whether to repair or replace, we usually ask one question first: how old is the ledger board? If it's original wood from 15+ years ago and hasn't been flashed properly, the answer is usually replace."
  • Soft, spongy, or visibly rotted framing lumber — posts, beams, or joists
  • Ledger board separation from the house or signs of moisture intrusion at the connection point
  • Deck is significantly undersized for how your family wants to use the space
  • Pressure-treated wood deck 20+ years old with widespread surface and structural deterioration
  • You want to upgrade to composite — sometimes a full rebuild is cleaner than a hybrid
  • Your deck fails a bounce test or feels unstable underfoot

The Replacement Process

What a Full Deck Replacement Includes

We handle the entire project from permit to final inspection — no subcontracting key trades to someone you've never met. Our crew tears out the old structure, inspects and addresses any house-side issues at the ledger connection, and builds new from properly set concrete footings.

Permit & Code

We pull the permit, schedule inspections, and ensure every footing depth and joist size meets Licking County code.

Full Tear-Out

Old deck, hardware, and debris hauled away. We leave your yard clean.

Footing Reset

New concrete footings poured to current frost depth requirements — no reusing old footings.

Ledger Flashing

Proper flashing and waterproofing where the deck connects to your home — the #1 failure point we see.

Material Choice

Composite (Trex, TimberTech, Fiberon) or pressure-treated wood — your call, we'll walk you through the tradeoffs.

Final Inspection

County building inspector signs off. We walk you through the completed deck before we call it done.

Deck Replacement FAQ

Common questions about full deck tear-out and replacement in Licking County.

If your deck has widespread rot in the structural framing — posts, ledger, beams, or rim joists — replacement is almost always more cost-effective than trying to sister in new lumber. A deck with cosmetic surface issues (weathered boards, a few soft spots) is a repair candidate. A deck where the frame feels spongy, bounces, or shows separation from the house is a replacement candidate. We'll tell you honestly which one you have.
For most homes in Licking County, a full deck replacement takes 3–6 days of active construction once permits are in hand and materials are staged. The permit process with Licking County typically runs 1–2 weeks. So plan on 3–4 weeks from first call to finished deck in a typical project.
Generally, no. Licking County requires footings to meet current frost depth requirements (typically 36–42 inches in this region). Older decks often have underpowered footings — tube forms barely below grade. When we replace a deck, we dig new footings to spec. This is non-negotiable for a permitted, code-compliant build.
A full deck replacement in Licking County typically runs $12,000–$28,000 for a 300–500 sq ft deck, depending on materials, size, height, and railing system. Pressure-treated wood costs less upfront. Composite costs more initially but has zero maintenance and a 25-year warranty. We'll give you pricing on both so you can make the right choice for your situation and timeline.
