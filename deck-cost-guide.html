Real pricing from the contractors who build decks in Licking County. No vague ranges, no national averages that don't apply here.

How We Think About Deck Pricing

We publish this cost guide because homeowners deserve honest numbers before they call anyone. The ranges online are almost always national averages that don't reflect what decks actually cost in central Ohio. We build decks in Licking County every week, and we know what materials, labor, and permits cost here right now.

Every number in this guide reflects installed cost — material, labor, permit, and cleanup. These are not material-only numbers.

When customers ask us "is this a good price?" we tell them the same thing: the price only matters if the build is right. A $12,000 deck with undersized joists and an improperly flashed ledger will cost you $20,000 to fix in year 8. Get the work spec'd correctly first, then compare prices.

Common Add-Ons and Their Cost

  • Cable railing system — $3,500–$8,000 depending on linear footage and post material (steel vs. aluminum)
  • Aluminum picket railing — $2,000–$5,000 — durable and low-maintenance alternative to wood rail
  • Attached cedar pergola — $4,500–$9,000 for a standard 12x16 structure
  • Freestanding pergola — $6,000–$14,000 depending on size and material
  • Built-in bench seating — $800–$2,500 per linear run
  • Stairs (per flight) — $1,200–$3,000 depending on height, width, and material
  • Outdoor kitchen rough-in — $2,000–$6,000 for blocking, blocking, and stub-outs (appliances separate)
  • Privacy lattice panels — $400–$1,200 per panel bay

What Drives Cost on Your Specific Project

The ranges above cover most Licking County deck projects, but your specific number depends on several factors we assess at the free estimate visit:

  • Deck height — Ground-level decks cost less than elevated decks. A walkout basement situation with 8–10 feet of drop requires taller posts, more bracing, and potentially under-deck framing for structural integrity.
  • Access — Tight side yards, fencing, and difficult site access can add 5–15% to labor cost.
  • Existing structure — If we're replacing an existing deck, tear-out and haul-away is $500–$2,000 depending on size.
  • Grade/terrain — Sloped lots, rocky soil, or high water table affects footing work and cost.
  • Ledger attachment — Brick, stone, EIFS, or composite siding requires additional flashing detail and sometimes more labor than standard wood-framed walls.
