About Us

Built for Ohio.
Built to Last.

We build decks in Licking County. That means concrete pier footings drilled below Ohio's frost line, hardware rated for ground contact, and composite materials chosen specifically because they handle freeze-thaw cycles without warping, cracking, or requiring a full reseal every other spring.

"In our experience with Licking County homes, the biggest mistake we see is inadequate footing depth. Ohio's frost line sits around 32 inches. A deck with shallow footings heaves and shifts — and eventually the whole structure has to come out. We do it right from the ground up."

Deck costs in Licking County vary significantly by material and size. A pressure-treated wood deck for a typical 12×16 to 16×20 space runs $8,000–$18,000. A composite Trex or TimberTech deck of the same size runs $18,000–$40,000 depending on railing style, features, and grade of material. Multi-level builds, pergolas, and outdoor kitchens are priced on top of those figures. We provide free, itemized written estimates — call us at {PHONE}.
Yes — most decks in Licking County require a permit. Attached decks, elevated decks over 30 inches off grade, and any deck over 200 square feet almost always require one. Requirements vary slightly by township. In our experience, the biggest headache for homeowners isn't getting the permit — it's not knowing which office to file with. We handle the entire permit application for our projects. See our Licking County Deck Permit Guide for more detail.
Most residential decks in Licking County take 3 to 7 business days to complete from first footing to last board. A straightforward ground-level wood deck can be done in 3 days. A larger composite build with pergola and cable railing may take 1 to 2 weeks. Permit approval timing varies — we account for that in your project schedule and communicate clearly throughout.
When customers ask us this, we tell them to think about the 10-year cost, not just the upfront number. A pressure-treated wood deck needs staining or sealing every 2–3 years — that's $500–$1,500 per application plus your time. A composite deck needs nothing beyond an occasional rinse. Over 10 years, the maintenance cost difference often closes the gap significantly. For Pataskala and New Albany homeowners planning to stay in their homes long-term, composite almost always wins. See our full Composite vs. Wood comparison guide.
It depends on the structure. If your deck boards are rotted but the framing and ledger are solid, board replacement or an overlay is a real option. If the joists are soft, the ledger is compromised, or the footings have shifted — that's a replacement. We inspect every deck honestly and tell you which it is before we quote anything. In our experience, about 60% of “I need deck repair” calls in Licking County turn into full replacements after inspection, because the problems run deeper than the surface.
