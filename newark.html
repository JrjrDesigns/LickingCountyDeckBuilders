Deck Building in Licking County's Largest City

Newark is home to roughly 50,000 people and a wide range of home styles — from the older bungalows and two-stories near downtown to the colonials and ranch-style homes on the west and south sides, built from the 1970s through today. In our experience with Newark homes, the deck needs vary a lot by neighborhood.

The older homes near the Granville Road and West Main Street corridors often have original pressure-treated decks from the late 1980s or 1990s that are at end-of-life — we see a lot of deck replacement work here. The newer subdivisions on the southwest side of Newark are where we see most of the new-build composite deck requests — larger lots, newer homes, homeowners investing in outdoor living from the start.

"Newark homeowners on the southeast side often ask about raised decks on their walkout lots. The elevation change in that part of the city creates a different set of structural requirements — and usually a more dramatic finished product."

We know Newark's building department and have pulled permits through the City of Newark dozens of times. Permit inspection scheduling is something we manage completely — you don't have to take time off work to meet the inspector.

Deck Building FAQ — Newark, Ohio

What Newark homeowners ask us most about deck projects.

Yes — Newark requires a building permit for any deck over 200 square feet or elevated more than 30 inches above grade. The City of Newark Building Department handles residential permits. We pull the permit and manage all inspections for you. This is included in every project we do in Newark.
A 300–400 sq ft pressure-treated deck in Newark typically runs $8,500–$14,000. The same size in composite (Trex or TimberTech) runs $14,000–$22,000. Multi-level decks, elevated structures, and premium railing systems add cost. We give you a written estimate broken down by materials, labor, and permit so you know exactly what you're paying for.
Both composite and pressure-treated wood handle Ohio's winters well when properly installed. Composite is increasingly the preferred choice in Licking County because it doesn't absorb moisture, doesn't check or splinter in the cold, and needs no annual sealing or staining. Pressure-treated is still the right call when budget is the primary constraint.
Active construction on a typical single-level deck in Newark takes 3–5 days. Permit processing with the City of Newark typically takes 1–2 weeks. Plan on approximately 3–4 weeks from your first call to a finished deck in a standard project timeline.
