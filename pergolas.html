Attached Pergolas

Bring Shade & Structure to Your Deck

An attached pergola is the single most popular addition we add to both new and existing decks. It creates a shaded zone for outdoor dining, adds architectural height, and gives the whole space definition — something a deck without overhead structure just doesn't have.

We see a lot of requests for pergolas in Licking County come after summer hits and homeowners realize their south- or west-facing deck is unusable in the afternoon. A pergola with the right rafter spacing or lattice coverage solves that. We'll walk you through the options at no cost during your site visit.

  • Attached directly to your home's ledger or existing deck posts
  • Cedar or composite rafters and beams — stained or painted to match your trim
  • Optional: privacy lattice panels, string light wiring, hanging planters
  • Available with louvered roof systems for full rain and sun control
Freestanding Pergolas

Create a Destination in Your Backyard

Not every pergola needs to attach to the house. Freestanding structures let you create a shaded seating or dining zone anywhere in your yard — over a patio, beside a pool, or at the far end of a large property. They're permitted and built the same way: footings, posts, beams, and rafters designed to handle Ohio weather.

"When customers ask about evening use of their outdoor space, we usually talk about two things: a pergola for shade and definition, and wiring for string lights. That combination does more for how much you use the space than any other upgrade."
Cedar

Naturally rot-resistant, stainable, beautiful grain — the classic choice for a backyard pergola.

Pressure-Treated

Budget-friendly, paintable, durable — great if you plan to cover with climbing plants or vines.

Composite

Matches your composite deck exactly. Zero maintenance, color-stable, won't warp or check.

Aluminum

Powder-coated, lightweight, and available with louvered roof panels for full weather control.

Pergola FAQ — Licking County

What homeowners ask us most about pergola construction.

Attached pergolas — anything connected to the house structure — almost always require a building permit in Licking County. Freestanding pergolas under certain square footage thresholds sometimes don't, depending on your municipality. We navigate this for you and pull any required permits.
Cedar is the most popular because it's naturally rot-resistant and looks great stained. If you already have a composite deck, we can match composite pergola components for a unified look. Aluminum louvered systems are the premium choice for homeowners who want adjustable sun and rain control year-round.
Yes — this is one of the most common projects we do. We assess your existing deck's structural condition first to make sure it can handle the additional load, then design the pergola to integrate with your existing post layout or add new posts if needed.
A standard 12x16 cedar attached pergola typically runs $4,500–$8,000. Freestanding structures and larger sizes cost more. Louvered aluminum systems start around $10,000. We give you free written pricing at the site visit so you can compare options without guessing.
We can rough in a dedicated circuit for string lights, a ceiling fan, or exterior fixtures as part of the pergola project. This requires an electrician for final connection — we coordinate with a licensed electrician or can work alongside yours.
Most pergola builds take 2–4 days of active construction. If we're adding it to an existing deck with no other structural work, we can often finish in 2 days. Permit timelines vary but typically run 1–2 weeks in Licking County.
