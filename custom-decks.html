Multi-Level Decks

When Your Yard Calls for More Than One Level

Licking County has a lot of topographic variation — especially in the southeastern portions of Newark, on walkout basement lots in Pataskala's newer subdivisions, and in the terrain around Granville. Multi-level decks are often the right design answer for these sites: they let you make use of the grade change rather than fight it.

"In our experience with walkout lot homes in Licking County, a two-level deck — main level off the first floor, lower level at grade — often costs less total than you'd expect because the lower level uses the grade itself as part of the structure. And the finished result is dramatically better than a single-level elevated platform."
  • Upper and lower levels connected by integrated staircase — no bolted-on afterthought stairs
  • Different materials possible on each level — composite upper, concrete or pavers lower
  • Built-in seating walls between levels double as safety rail and design feature
  • Permit engineering accounts for multi-level load requirements — no guessing on joist sizing
Cable Railing & Premium Rail Systems

Railing That Doesn't Block Your View

The most common custom upgrade request we get is cable railing — particularly on elevated decks or decks that overlook a yard, woods, or water feature. Cable railing preserves the sightline in a way that no picket or glass system quite matches.

Built-In Features

Deck Features Built In — Not Bolted On

The difference between a well-designed custom deck and an afterthought add-on is whether the features were planned into the structure from the start. Built-in seating, planters, an outdoor kitchen platform, or a bar area all require structural planning — framing layout, blocking, and load calculation done before concrete is poured.

We design these features into the deck from the beginning, not as add-ons that compromise the structural layout. When you work with us on a custom deck, we talk through every feature at the design stage so nothing is left to figure out later.

  • Built-in bench seating — framed into the deck structure, not surface-mounted
  • Outdoor kitchen platform and blocking for grill, side burner, and refrigerator
  • Planter boxes integrated into railing or perimeter framing
  • Electrical blocking for string lights, ceiling fans, and exterior fixtures
  • Hot tub platform with proper structural reinforcement for 3,000+ lb wet weight
